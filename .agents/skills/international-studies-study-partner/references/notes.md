# Capture, transform and synthesize notes

Choose the operation the student requested. These operations have different fidelity requirements; a neat format should not conceal lost distinctions or added claims.

## Capture / 記錄筆記

Capture material the student actually supplied: typed notes, selected conversation, accessible transcript, lecture slides or extracts. Recording notes does not imply microphone access, attendance at a lecture or continuous background capture.

Preserve substantive statements, questions, quotations and source locations. Lightly normalize formatting or obvious transcription issues; keep ambiguity visible. Do not expand an unfinished fragment into a factual claim. Use “Unclear in the supplied notes” or “To check” for consequential gaps.

If converting Chinese rough notes to English, preserve the original fragment when its meaning is uncertain. A translation is not a new source. Distinguish what the lecturer said, what the student wondered and what the assistant adds; do not attribute unlabeled fragments to the lecturer by assumption.

Save the original input or a link to it alongside the cleaned capture when feasible and within the requested scope. If the original exists as a page, preserve it rather than copying or replacing it unnecessarily.

## Transform / 轉化筆記

Default to organized English course notes, a concept table or a comparison table. Select the format by the material:

| Material | Useful structure |
|---|---|
| A lecture or several related fragments | Central question → themes → arguments/examples → open questions |
| Terms and definitions | Concept → meaning in this context → distinction/example → source |
| Competing theories or institutions | A shared question and comparison dimensions → positions → evidence/limits → source |
| Historical developments | Chronology where dates are known, plus separate explanation of change and continuity |

Do not impose tables on an argument that is clearer in prose. Preserve source caveats, counterarguments, uncertainty and dates. Deduplicate genuinely repeated points, but keep conflicting accounts distinct. Reorganization is not permission to fill evidence gaps with general knowledge.

When the input contains a likely factual error, retain what it says as a source claim or uncertainty. Verify before presenting a correction as established, and put corrections in a separate “Clarifications / corrections” section. Do not silently overwrite the student's original wording.

A before/after audit should establish that substantive points remain represented and that new explanatory material is labeled. A transformed note can become shorter, but must not silently lose a counterargument or change “can” into “always.”

## Synthesize / 總結筆記

Use the exact pages, topics, course or date/week scope requested. Inspect the relevant notes, not just their titles. For a weekly or “all notes” request, retrieve the records within scope and follow pagination when available; state which materials are missing or inaccessible. A search sample is not a complete course archive.

Build the summary around shared questions, relationships and distinctions. Link important statements to the contributing notes, retain conflicts and note where an AI connection goes beyond the sources. Multiple notes derived from one article count as one underlying source, not independent evidence.

A concise synthesis can contain a central takeaway, thematic points, a comparison where useful, and unresolved questions. Do not silently turn it into a revision quiz, a reading list, or an essay. Keep the original notes available and separate from the synthesis; update an existing synthesis when that is the requested target.

## Reusable page layout

Use this as a flexible layout, not a form to fill with invented information:

- **Source and scope:** course if known; lecture/reading/topic; material actually read; source links and locations. Unknown course week or author can remain unset.
- **Core question / overview:** orient the reader.
- **Structured notes:** themes, concepts and arguments; select an appropriate table if helpful.
- **Clarifications:** brief Chinese support and explicitly labeled AI explanations or verified corrections, only when needed.
- **Open questions:** genuine gaps or the student's questions, not a generic checklist.
- **Sources / related notes:** direct source links and related pages used in this output.

In chat these can be standard Markdown. For Notion writes convert to its fetched Markdown specification and the destination's existing structure.
