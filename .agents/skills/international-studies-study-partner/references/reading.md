# English reading guide

## Establish the available text

Read the supplied article, chapter, excerpt, file or linked page. Identify title, author, version and year only where visible or verified. A title alone does not establish the contents. If a link cannot be read, say so and request the relevant text while helping with any material already available.

State the coverage briefly: full text, specified pages, excerpt, abstract, or notes about an unavailable original. For PDFs, distinguish printed page numbers from viewer page positions where needed; inspect original pages for diagrams or unreliable extraction. For web text, use actual headings; for a supplied untitled excerpt, use descriptive paragraph locations and label any paragraph numbering as added for reference.

## Explain the article's structure

Give a short overview first, then expand according to the requested depth. A normal reading note can contain:

1. **Central question and answer** — what the text is trying to explain or establish.
2. **Argument map** — how its main steps connect, rather than a disconnected list of topics.
3. **Support** — evidence, examples, textual interpretation or reasoning that the author uses.
4. **Key concepts** — English definitions in the author's context; add Chinese explanations where needed.
5. **Difficult passages** — selected only when useful, with an English paraphrase and concise Chinese explanation.
6. **Qualifications and open questions** — the source's limits, followed by clearly attributed analytical questions if requested.

Omit sections that do not help this reading. A request for a brief summary does not require a full note or critique. Match a supplied word limit; otherwise choose length in proportion to the text and the student's purpose.

Adapt to genre. Empirical articles need the question, research design, result and limitations. Philosophical or theoretical works need premises, definitions and inferences; do not invent samples or findings. Historical chapters need chronology, evidence and interpretation. News and opinion need their genre and attribution to remain visible.

## Preserve meaning

- Retain negation, scope, uncertainty, conditions and the difference between possibility and inevitability.
- Identify whose view is being reported. An author presenting an objection is not necessarily endorsing it.
- Attribute contested interpretations; do not present them as settled definitions.
- Use quotations only when their exact wording matters and the text is actually available. Keep location and edition information; paraphrases are not quotations.
- Explain pronouns, logical connectors and specialized terms that obstruct understanding. Do not list every unfamiliar English word by default.
- Put external context under a distinct label such as “Additional context,” with sources when applicable. Do not let it inflate the apparent contents of the article.

## Handoff

For a Notion save, use a title such as “Reading — Author — Short title” when those details are known. Carry over the reading scope, original source link and important page/section locations. A stored reading note should remain distinguishable from the original article and from later course syntheses.

When a later task needs an important direct quotation or a disputed detail, return to the source. Do not treat a previous AI summary as having verified the original.
